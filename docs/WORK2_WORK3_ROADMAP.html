The shape
Both works get all three lanes — learn, build, write — from week 1. The parallelism is bought two ways: Work 3's blocking waits become Work 2 sprints, and Vikas is instrumented rather than built.

The version of this plan that fails is the one that slices six build hours into three and three. Deep build work doesn't divide — three hours a week of experimentation finishes nothing in either paper. So the split is temporal, not proportional: Work 3 owns the build lane by default, and Work 2 takes it whole during the weeks Work 3 is blocked on someone or something else.

Work 3 submits week 17. Work 2 has a complete first draft by week 24, with its baseline measurements done before Work 3 even leaves your hands.

Why this is affordable now

Two things make the parallel version work

Work 3 is full of dead time Weeks 8–9 the annotators are working independently — you are waiting. Week 12 the Llama fine-tune is running on Lightning — you are waiting. Weeks 15–16 the supervisor has the manuscript — you are waiting. That is roughly five weeks of already-budgeted build hours where Work 3 physically cannot advance. Those weeks are Work 2's, and taking them costs Work 3 nothing.
Vikas is not greenfield The Work 2 testbed is a 45-agent system already running in production. The build work is capability manifests, signed tool calls, and an audit log added to code you own and operate daily — not a simulator written from scratch. Much of it is work the platform arguably needs regardless. That is why Work 2 build can start in month 1 instead of month 5.
The honest cost: Work 3's submission moves from week 16 to week 17, and the weekly budget goes from 12 to 14 hours. If 12 is a hard ceiling, keep it — Work 3 then lands week 19 and Work 2's draft week 26. Both are still far better than the serial path.

Sequencing logic

Why Work 2 builds in this order

Each increment is independently useful and produces a paper artefact, so an interrupted month never leaves you with a half-thing that proves nothing:

wk 2 · harness
A server and client you fully control. Everything later is measured against this, and it doubles as the artefact you release.
wk 5–9 · instrumentation
Manifests, signed calls, audit log. Without the log you cannot observe an attack succeeding — so this must precede the attacks, not follow them.
wk 11–14 · attacks + baseline
One attack per STRIDE category, then the undefended success rate. The baseline is worthless if measured after you've hardened anything.
wk 15–20 · defence + delta
Enforcement, re-run, and the cost of enforcement. The delta is the contribution; the cost is what makes it credible.
Risk

What actually kills this plan

Two papers, both at 90%
The real failure mode of parallel work. Defence: only Work 3 has a submission date before week 17. Whenever the two collide, Work 3 wins and Work 2 falls back to its 2h reading floor — never to zero, never above the floor.
The second annotator
Load-bearing for contribution #2 and the one thing whose timing you don't control. If unconfirmed by week 3, Gate B slips and the week 8–9 sprint arrives early instead — which is survivable, but only if you notice.
Instrumenting production
Vikas is live. Signed tool calls and enforcement on a running 45-agent system can break outreach. Build behind a flag, shadow-mode first, and never let a Work 2 experiment page you at 2am during a Work 3 gate week.
Work 1 revision returns
A second round on COMNET-D-858858 could land any week. Treat it as a two-week full stop and push everything right — do not try to absorb it into a 14-hour week.
